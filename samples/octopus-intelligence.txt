Octopuses have three hearts and blue blood, but the truly strange part is how they think. About two-thirds of an octopus's neurons live not in its brain but in its eight arms, each one able to taste, touch, and even solve simple problems on its own without instructions from the brain. Cut off from the body, an arm can still reach for food and pull away from a threat. Octopuses can also change the color and texture of their skin in less than a second, using thousands of pigment cells called chromatophores, to vanish against coral, mimic a flounder, or flash a warning at a rival. Despite living only one to two years, they solve puzzles, open jars, escape tanks through gaps barely wider than their beaks, and appear to play. Their intelligence evolved on a branch of the tree of life that split from ours over five hundred million years ago, making them the closest thing on Earth to meeting an alien mind.
